# Literature search: 4_entry_level_jobs (2026-10-15)

**Bottom line:** The international work now has a clear benchmark. Stanford's revised "Canaries" paper (Aug 2026) finds US workers aged 22–25 in AI-exposed jobs are 19% below trend, and the cause is less hiring rather than layoffs. For India, the closest direct evidence is ICRIER's survey of 651 IT firms (Feb 2026): 55% of firms report fewer entry-level hires, and 42% report more mid-level hires. That lines up closely with your "output growing, entry rung narrowing" claim. The main pushback comes from job-portal data (Naukri, LinkedIn), which shows fresher demand rising in early 2026, and from US unemployment studies that find no effect yet. You should address both.

**Ranked items**

1. **"AI and Jobs: This Time Is No Different – A case study of India's IT sector"**. ICRIER (Mishra, Kedia, Reddy et al.), Feb 2026. https://icrier.org/publications/ai-and-jobs-this-time-is-no-different-a-case-study-of-indias-it-sector/ (PDF: https://www.icrier.org/pdf/AI-and-Jobs.pdf)
   - Data: survey of 651 IT firms in 10 cities, Nov 2025–Jan 2026, plus interviews.
   - Finding: about two-thirds of firms say hiring fell after they adopted AI. By level: entry 55% down vs 35% up; mid-level 42% up vs 25% down; senior 82% no change. No mass layoffs.
   - Relevance: the strongest India overlap. Cite it, and note that OpenAI funded it.

2. **"Canaries in the Coal Mine?"** (revised). Brynjolfsson, Chandar, Chen (Stanford Digital Economy Lab), 12 Aug 2026. https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recen
   - Data: ADP payroll through June 2026.
   - Finding: ages 22–25 in exposed jobs are 19% below trend (13% in the 2025 version), and the gap is still widening. It works through hiring, not separations, and shows up where AI substitutes for work. No economy-wide losses. The authors say the effect weakens once education is controlled for.
   - Relevance: the main benchmark. US only.

3. **"Generative AI as Seniority-Biased Technological Change"**. Hosseini & Lichtinger, latest version 6 May 2026. https://raw.githack.com/s-mahdihosseini/Website/b99fa2962dcc4c165bbc3c41dd283afa99c5ccfb/GenAI_SenBTC_May_2026.pdf
   - Data: Revelio résumés and job postings, about 285k US firms and 65M workers.
   - Finding: at firms that adopted GenAI, junior headcount fell from 2023Q1 while senior headcount kept rising. The cause is fewer hires. Graduates of mid-tier colleges are hit hardest.
   - Relevance: a benchmark for a firm-level age mix. The mid-tier result is a useful parallel to your NIRF tiers.

4. **Nomura on AI and jobs in Asia**. Varma & Toh, Aug 2026, as reported in Business Today. https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/artificial-intelligence/story/india-adds-2-6-ai-jobs-for-every-role-lost-but-a-widening-skills-gap-threatens-freshers-report-548182-2026-08-09
   - Data: 69 company cases. India: 83,100 AI-related hires vs 31,921 losses.
   - Finding: calls India "ground zero" and describes a "two-tier labour market". Cuts land in support/BPO, hiring goes to experienced AI talent. Also cites Xpheno: net IT hiring fell from about 600k (FY22) to 140k (FY26).
   - Relevance: overlaps with your piece. Note it counts company cases, not a census.

5. **South Asia Economic Update: "Adopting AI for Growth"**. World Bank, Oct 2026. https://www.worldbank.org/southasiadevelopment
   - Finding: hiring in BPO/ICT has slowed since GenAI arrived even though exports keep growing. Flags "reshoring" of software and support work.
   - The Oct 2025 update: business-services postings −35% and wages −8% relative to trend. https://www.medianama.com/2025/10/223-ai-job-loss-south-asia-world-bank/
   - Relevance: strongly supports your "output up, hiring flat" framing. I only saw press coverage of the Oct 2026 text.

6. **"The AI Exposure of Export-Linked Jobs"**. Kruse & Yu, World Development Report 2026 background paper, Jul 2026. https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/9df559d23a6d80b3dfa2c4bc808f3a20-0050062026/original/The-AI-Exposure-of-Export-Linked-Jobs.pdf
   - Data: labour-force surveys plus input-output tables for 26 economies.
   - Finding: India is about 50% above trend in the share of export-linked jobs at risk of AI substitution, because it specialises in business services. Expected effect: losses within the sector, moderate in aggregate.
   - Its references point to new offshoring papers: Betai & Chen 2026 (AI reduces outsourcing), Stapleton & O'Kane 2026 (AI raises it), Bastos & Nayyar 2026 (no effect).

7. **State of Working India 2026**. Azim Premji University, Mar 2026, as reported in Business Today. https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/jobs/story/youth-in-labour-market-graduate-unemployment-remains-high-in-india-520932-2026-03-17
   - Data: PLFS and CMIE.
   - Finding: about 40% unemployment among graduates aged 15–25. Only 7% of young male graduates get permanent salaried work within a year.
   - Relevance: partly contradicts you. Graduate unemployment is very high in level even if it is falling in trend. Address it.

8. **Infosys under-30 share at a 15-year low**. Mint analysis of filings, reported by People Matters, 1 Jun 2026. https://www.peoplematters.in/news/business/infosys-sees-lowest-proportion-of-under-30-employees-in-15-years-50011
   - Finding: 50.7% of employees were 30 or under in FY26, down from 53% the year before.
   - Earlier Mint pieces, which I saw only in search summaries, report TCS falling from 59% to 47.7% between FY22 and FY25.
   - Relevance: directly overlaps your under-30 chart. Credit Mint.

9. **Contradictions from job-portal data:**
   - Naukri JobSpeak: fresher (0–3 yrs) postings +8% YoY in Jan 2026 and +17% in Feb, with IT flat. https://naukri.com/blog/naukri-jobspeak-white-collar-hiring-opens-2026-with-3-yoy-growth-driven-by-non-it-sectors-and-fresher-hiring/
   - LinkedIn Grad's Guide 2026: India entry-level hiring +168% from 2023 to 2025. The metric is undefined. https://peoplematters.in/news/strategic-hr/entry-level-hiring-climbs-168percent-as-ai-roles-and-internships-gain-ground-linkedin-49290
   - Both count postings or platform activity, not hires at the big firms.

10. **Null results from the US and Denmark:**
    - Fairlie & Wu, NBER w35796 / IZA DP 18945, Sep 2026. CPS data: recent-graduate unemployment did not spike in summer 2026. https://www.nber.org/papers/w35796
    - Humlum & Vestergaard, NBER w33777, revised Mar 2026. Danish administrative data: no effect on hiring, including for early-career workers. https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33777/w33777.pdf
    - These parallel your "graduate unemployment still falling" point.

11. **Other benchmarks:**
    - Dallas Fed (Jan–Mar 2026): small effects on young workers in AI-exposed jobs, and rising returns to experience. https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/system-research-dallas-fed/2026/03/young-workers-employment-drops-occupations-high-ai-exposure/
    - Revelio: US entry-level postings −35% since 2023. https://www.reveliolabs.com/blog/fast-company-entry-level-hiring
    - Stanford AI Index 2026: US software developers aged 22–25 about −20%. I saw only secondary coverage of this.

**Datasets you may be missing**
- **EPFO** net payroll by industry and age. "Computer-related services" can be cut for ages 18–25 from the monthly releases (one compilation is on Dataful).
- **CMIE CPHS**, which State of Working India uses for graduate job transitions.
- **Naukri JobSpeak**, monthly series with a 0–3 years experience band.
- **Revelio and LinkedIn**: both have Indian profiles, so an India version of the Hosseini–Lichtinger design is possible.
- **Xpheno and TeamLease Digital** fresher-hiring estimates.
- **Microdata or follow-up for the ICRIER survey.**

I found no academic study that uses PLFS or EPFO specifically for young IT workers. That gap is what makes your PLFS relative-pay result new.
