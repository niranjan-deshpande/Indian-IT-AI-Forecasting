# Literature search: 1_think_tanks_academic (2026-10-15)

**Bottom line:** I found no think-tank, policy or academic piece that does what ours does: put firm results, RBI and balance-of-payments exports, GCCs, mid-tier firms, PLFS, fresher hiring and pay, and prices into one health check. Each piece below covers one or two of those. Several make our headline point, that output is growing while hiring falls behind and the entry level carries the squeeze, but from a single source: one firm survey, BoP exports, job postings, or scenario models. None of them uses quarterly results from TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, Wipro, TechM or LTIMindtree. That gives us a real gap to fill, but we should cite the Economic Survey, ICRIER and the World Bank as close neighbours.

**Most relevant, ranked**

1. **ICRIER, "AI and Jobs: This Time Is No Different."** Mishra, Kedia, Reddy, Fernandez, Khanduri. February 2026, with support from OpenAI. https://icrier.org/publications/ai-and-jobs-this-time-is-no-different-a-case-study-of-indias-it-sector/
   - **Data:** a survey of 651 IT firms in 10 cities (Nov 2025–Jan 2026), plus interviews.
   - **Claim:** no large-scale job losses. Hiring has moderated, with the slowdown at entry level, more demand for mid-level staff and stable senior levels.
   - **Overlap:** the closest on jobs. It has no firm financials, exports, PLFS or pay data.
   - **Contradicts us:** it says "total employment in the sector has continued to grow." That cuts against "stopped adding jobs," so we need to deal with it head-on.

2. **Economic Survey 2025-26, Ministry of Finance (Jan 2026).** I read both PDFs directly.
   - **Ch. 7, Box VII.2** (https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap07.pdf): uses quarterly RBI BoP data, FY18 to Q2 FY26. It compares AI-exposed services with less-exposed ones from Q2 FY23 and finds AI-exposed exports up about 39.5%, led by software and business services.
   - **Ch. 14** (https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap14.pdf): a time-series model on US professional, business and information services employment. It finds that since Dec 2022, jobs respond less to output growth.
   - **Overlap:** strong on the "output up, jobs lag" idea. But the jobs evidence is from the **US**, not India, and there are no firm, GCC or entry-level data.
   - **Caution:** press summaries say the Survey shows export gains "not translating into jobs" in India. I did not find that tested in the text.

3. **World Bank, South Asia Development Update "Jobs, AI, and Trade."** October 2025, plus Ohnsorge slides from Feb 2026. https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/e59d0c80ed5c4a928630c9d2295ea0ad-0360012025/original/SADU25b-Full-Version-10-3-2025.pdf
   - **Data:** Lightcast job postings (Aug 2020–Mar 2025), RBI BPO exports, and labour-force microdata.
   - **Claim:** after ChatGPT, BPO postings and wages fell while India's BPO exports surged. Hiring is slowing in entry-level jobs that AI can substitute for, with younger upper-mid-skilled workers most exposed.
   - **Overlap:** this is our thesis in miniature, but for the region, with BPO-heavy evidence and postings rather than employment counts.
   - **Dataset we lack:** Lightcast postings.

4. **World Bank, India Development Update "India and Artificial Intelligence: Seizing the Development Opportunity."** October 2026. https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/099455510022634209 (I verified it only through Policy Edge coverage; the report page would not render.)
   - **Data:** a firm AI-adoption survey (about 23% of formal firms use AI), exposure measures (22% of employment highly exposed), and it cites the ICRIER survey: 55% of IT firms report lower entry-level hiring. Coverage elsewhere also gives GCC employment of 2.36m.
   - **Overlap:** economy-wide; it frames the change as shifts in hiring, not job loss.

5. **ORF, "Capability in the Age of AI: India's GCCs and the Future of White-Collar Work."** Roy Bardhan and Bhowmick, Special Report 312, 16 June 2026. https://orfonline.org/research/capability-in-the-age-of-ai-india-s-gccs-and-the-future-of-white-collar-work
   - **Data:** Nasscom-Zinnov GCC 2026 figures, EY GCC Pulse, IHD-ILO/PLFS, and its own scenarios.
   - **Claim:** AI "may reduce entry-level absorption even if aggregate GCC output continues to grow." It estimates gross displacement of 40k–151k by 2030.
   - **Companion:** "Evaluating the AI Threat to India's IT Model" (29 June 2026), https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/evaluating-the-ai-threat-to-india-s-it-model. It argues revenue can grow while jobs shrink and that PLFS/EPFO can't detect this.
   - **Overlap:** GCCs only.

6. **NITI Aayog, NASSCOM and BCG, "Roadmap for Job Creation in the AI Economy."** October 2025. https://niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-10/Roadmap_for_Job_Creation_in_the_AI_Economy.pdf
   - **Claim:** two scenarios. Tech-services jobs fall from 7.5–8m to about 6m by 2031, or rise to 10m.
   - **Overlap:** forward-looking scenarios, not a reading of current data.

7. **K P Sunny, "Artificial Intelligence and Future of India's IT Companies."** EPW Engage, 1 July 2026. https://www.epw.in/engage/article/artificial-intelligence-and-future-indias-it
   - **Data:** market caps of TCS, Infosys, HCLTech, Persistent, Coforge and LTIMindtree, plus Nasscom and ICRIER figures.
   - **Claim:** the model of growing revenue by adding headcount is breaking. AI has cut entry-level hiring rather than causing layoffs.
   - **Overlap:** the only one that touches firms, but through stock prices, not quarterly results.

8. **Great Lakes CFER WP5, "AI, Automation and India's Workforce."** Mahambare, Jadhav, Venugopalan, with a foreword by CEA Nageswaran. October 2026. https://greatlakes.edu.in/chennai/assets/greatlakes/images/GLCFER-WP5.pdf
   - **Data:** PLFS 2025 versus NSS 2011-12.
   - **Claim:** occupational exposure; 39.4% of employed 15–24-year-olds are in high-routine jobs.
   - **Overlap:** economy-wide, PLFS only. Useful for checking our PLFS numbers.

9. **Azim Premji University, State of Working India 2026.** March 2026. https://azimpremjifoundation.org/state-of-working-india-2026-indias-young-workforce-is-growing-and-getting-more-educated/
   - **Claim:** graduate unemployment is about 40% for under-25s. It is not about IT, but it is context for the entry rung.

10. **Lower relevance:**
    - ICRIER policy brief "Interactions of AI with India's Labour Market" (Malik and Jain, Feb 2026): uses KLEMS and PLFS. https://icrier.org/pdf/Interactions-of-Artificial-Intelligence-with-India-s-Labour-Market.pdf
    - Betai and Chen (GWU), "Automation without Borders" (Jan 2026): uses online-freelance-platform data and finds outsourced contracts to developing countries down 34% at mean AI exposure. https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/2770aa5def57e42a6d2449c90d6cf974-0070062026/original/10-Chen.pdf

**Not found:** nothing relevant in this window from Carnegie India, CSEP, Takshashila, IDFC Institute, NCAER or Brookings. I also found no RBI Bulletin article, no 2026 IMF Article IV (the latest is Nov 2025, without an AI focus), and no India-specific IT paper on NBER, arXiv or IZA.

**Contradictions to address:**
- ICRIER says employment is still growing.
- Industry figures relayed in coverage (outside my lane, not verified) put Nasscom's FY26 headcount at +2.3%, about 135k. They also show the top five firms net −7k. So "stopped adding jobs" holds for the big firms but may not hold sector-wide, depending on what counts as "the sector."
- The Economic Survey's US result shows employment rising after Dec 2022, just less responsive to output.

**Datasets others use that we may lack:**
- Lightcast job postings (World Bank)
- ICRIER's 651-firm survey
- Nasscom-Zinnov GCC landscape data
- India KLEMS
- World Bank firm AI-adoption survey
- Online-freelance-platform contract microdata (Betai and Chen)
